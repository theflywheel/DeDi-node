# One-click deploy

[![Deploy on Railway](https://railway.com/button.svg)](https://railway.com/deploy/mfW-Zh)

Deploying gives you a Postgres with a volume and a DeDi node pulling
[`flywheelai/dedi-node`](https://hub.docker.com/r/flywheelai/dedi-node), on a
public domain, signing a checkpoint every 30 seconds. It asks you nothing — the
database password, the admin password, the log origin and the public URL are all
derived at deploy time.

Your node mints its own Ed25519 identity key on first boot and keeps it in its
own database. Nothing has to be generated beforehand and handed to it.

## What you get

Within a couple of minutes:

    https://<your-domain>/                          the explorer
    https://<your-domain>/healthz                   status, including database reachability
    https://<your-domain>/dedi/log/checkpoint       a signed checkpoint over your log
    https://<your-domain>/.well-known/dedi.index.json   your signed publication manifest

The checkpoint's first line is your log's origin, which is your own domain. That
is the thing anyone verifying you — or witnessing you — checks against, so it is
tied to the deployment rather than to us.

To hand someone your verifier key:

    railway logs -s dedi-node | grep -A1 'verifier key'

## It serves reads, and accepts no writes

A fresh node is read-only, and that is the intended starting state rather than a
missing step. The write plane only exists once `DEDI_PUBLISHER_KEYS` is set, and
that key is generated by you:

    dedid pubkeygen -kid my-key -namespace my-namespace

It prints the private key to keep and the `DEDI_PUBLISHER_KEYS` line to set on
the service. Until you do, `/admin` is not merely locked — it is not routed at
all, and answers 404.

`DEDI_ADMIN_PASSWORD` is already set for you, to a value Railway generated for
your deployment alone. It gates the console the moment your publisher key brings
it into existence, which matters because that moment is exactly when nobody
remembers to go back and add a password. Read it from the Railway variables
panel; it is not written down anywhere else, including here.

## Joining a network

A node on its own is tamper-*evident* to anyone holding its checkpoints. It
becomes tamper-evident to people who are not watching it the moment another node
witnesses it: set `DEDI_WITNESS_TARGET_URL` (ending in `/dedi`),
`DEDI_WITNESS_TARGET_KEY` and `DEDI_WITNESS_TARGET_ORIGIN` and your node will
poll that one, verify its consistency proofs, and record its verdicts in its own
`_witness` namespace. `DEDI_PEERS` makes your explorer show the network you
belong to.

See [deployment-modes.md](deployment-modes.md) for witnessing, ledger anchoring
and clustering.

## Checking it conforms

The node you just deployed can be measured against the standard by a suite that
was not written to flatter it:

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD:/work" flywheelai/dedi-conformance:v0.0.1 \
      --manifest /work/dedi-conformance.json

It only issues GETs and needs no credentials. See
[conformance.md](conformance.md).

## Changing what the button deploys

The template is generated from a real Railway project, not written as a file, so
the project is the source of truth. `scripts/railway_template.py` builds that
project reproducibly — read its comments before editing, particularly the reason
every value in it is an interpolation rather than a literal.

    scripts/railway_template.py --build
    scripts/railway_template.py --generate <project-id>
